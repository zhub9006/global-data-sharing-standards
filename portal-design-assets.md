## Overview

This file contains a curated selection of high-resolution artworks from the Metropolitan Museum of Art spanning **World Cultures** and **Illuminated Manuscript** categories. Each entry includes full metadata and a direct image URL for use in portal design, presentations, and documentation.

### 1. The Jabach Family

| Field          | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| **Artist**     | Charles Le Brun (French, Paris 1619–1690 Paris) |
| **Department** | European Paintings |
| **Medium**     | Oil on canvas |
| **Dimensions** | 110 1/4 × 129 1/8 in. (280 × 328 cm) |
| **Credit Line**| Purchase, Mrs. Charles Wrightsman Gift, in honor of Keith Christiansen, 2014 |
| **Tags**       | Interiors, Boys, Girls, Infants, Men, Self-portraits, Women, Dogs, Family |
| **Object ID**  | 626692 |

**High-Resolution Image URL:**
https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/ep/original/DP352819.jpg

**Description:**
A monumental family portrait by Charles Le Brun, one of the leading painters of the French Baroque era. The work depicts the Jabach family in an opulent interior, showcasing Le Brun's mastery of composition, light, and aristocratic portraiture. Its extraordinary scale (over 9 feet wide) and rich detail make it a compelling anchor image for any culturally rich portal design.

### 2. The Fieschi Morgan Staurotheke

| Field          | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| **Department** | Medieval Art |
| **Medium**     | Gilded silver, gold, enamel worked in cloisonné, and niello |
| **Dimensions** | Overall (with lid): 1 1/16 x 4 1/16 x 2 13/16 in. (2.7 x 10.3 x 7.1 cm) |
| **Credit Line**| Gift of J. Pierpont Morgan, 1917 |
| **Tags**       | Christ, Virgin Mary, Crucifixion, Saints |
| **Object ID**  | 472562 |

**High-Resolution Image URL:**
https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/md/original/DT119.jpg

**Description:**
This Byzantine reliquary box (staurotheke) is a masterpiece of medieval metalwork. Crafted in gilded silver with intricate cloisonné enamel, it is among the finest examples of Byzantine goldsmithing in any public collection. Its jewel-like imagery of Christ, the Virgin Mary, and saints offers a powerful visual vocabulary for themes of heritage, craft, and cross-cultural exchange.

### 3. Four Gospels in Armenian

| Field          | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| **Department** | Medieval Art |
| **Medium**     | Tempera and gold on paper; stamped leather binding |
| **Dimensions** | Overall: 11 1/16 x 7 5/8 x 3 3/8 in. (28.1 x 19.4 x 8.5 cm) |
| **Credit Line**| Purchase, Fletcher Fund, Hagop Kevorkian Fund Gift, in memory of Hagop Kevorkian, Tianaderrah Foundation, B.H. Breslauer Foundation, Aso O. Tavitian, Karen Bedrosian Richardson, Elizabeth Mugar Eveillard and Arax Simsarian Gifts and funds from various donors, 2010 |
| **Tags**       | Men, Angels, Christ, Virgin Mary, Nativity |
| **Object ID**  | 478665 |

**High-Resolution Image URL:**
https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/md/original/DP228809.jpg

**Description:**
This Armenian Gospel book is a luminous example of medieval manuscript illumination. Produced with tempera and gold on paper, it features vivid miniatures of the Nativity, angels, and Gospel scenes. The stamped leather binding and the richness of its gilded imagery make it an exceptional reference for design work exploring themes of sacred text, cultural heritage, and the global art of the book.

### 4. Self-portrait — Simon Bening

| Field          | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| **Artist**     | Simon Bening (Netherlandish, Ghent (?) 1483/84–1561 Bruges) |
| **Department** | Robert Lehman Collection |
| **Medium**     | Tempera and gold leaf on parchment |
| **Dimensions** | 3 3/8 x 2 1/4 in. (8.5 x 5.7 cm) |
| **Credit Line**| Robert Lehman Collection, 1975 |
| **Tags**       | Men, Self-portraits |
| **Object ID**  | 459254 |

**High-Resolution Image URL:**
https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/rl/original/DT3622.jpg

**Description:**
Simon Bening was the last great master of Flemish manuscript illumination. This intimate self-portrait — executed in tempera and gold leaf on parchment — is a remarkable document of artistic identity from the twilight of the illuminated manuscript tradition. Its extraordinary detail rewards close inspection and serves as a poignant symbol of craftsmanship and personal expression across cultures.

### 5. Hebrew Bible

| Field          | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| **Department** | The Cloisters |
| **Medium**     | Ink, tempera, and gold on parchment; leather binding |
| **Dimensions** | 476 folios: 9 5/16 × 7 15/16 in. (23.7 × 20.1 cm); Binding: 9 15/16 × 8 3/4 × 4 1/4 in. (25.2 × 22.3 × 10.8 cm) |
| **Credit Line**| The Cloisters Collection, 2018 |
| **Object ID**  | 773026 |

**High-Resolution Image URL:**
https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/cl/original/DP-15507-001.jpg

**Description:**
This Hebrew Bible, acquired by The Cloisters in 2018, is an exceptional example of medieval Jewish manuscript production. With 476 parchment folios richly decorated in ink, tempera, and gold, and bound in a beautifully preserved leather cover, it stands as a testament to the global tradition of the sacred book. Its visual richness and cultural significance make it ideal for portal contexts celebrating the diversity of human knowledge and heritage.
